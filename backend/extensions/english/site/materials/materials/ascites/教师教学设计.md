# Case Answer Key · Key Clinical Reasoning Steps

## 1. Why screen for infection first

Hospitalized for recently worsening ascites, with abdominal discomfort and renal dysfunction; absence of high fever and a normal blood WBC count do not exclude SBP. The absolute ascitic fluid PMN count, rather than a positive culture, is the key to timely action.

## 2. Why this should not be directly equated with hepatorenal syndrome

A creatinine increase of 32 μmol/L within 20 hours confirms AKI. Recent inadequate intake, self-increased diuretic dosing, and infection may coexist. Urine sediment and ultrasound help exclude alternative causes, but normal results alone cannot establish HRS. Rapid recovery followed diuretic withdrawal and treatment of infection; the final diagnosis must not state confirmed HRS-AKI. [S5]

## 3. Why treatment is staged

On day 1, control infection, withhold diuretics, and assess circulatory status. Perform paracentesis after infection and renal function improve, then resume low-dose diuretics. Calculate albumin for SBP using estimated dry weight; calculate albumin for paracentesis using the volume drained. These must not be conflated as “give albumin whenever albumin is low.” [S3,S4]

## 4. Why not currently use TIPS

Refractory ascites has not been established after adequate, tolerated treatment and verification of adherence; the current episode has improved. TIPS is an option for recurrent refractory ascites and requires assessment of cardiopulmonary status, liver function, infection, and encephalopathy risk. Transplant evaluation may begin after decompensation rather than waiting until all treatments have failed. [S2,S6]

## 5. Why follow-up is still needed after discharge

AKI has resolved, but hyponatremia, hypoalbuminemia, portal hypertension, and chronic liver disease persist. After SBP, discuss secondary prophylaxis, antiviral treatment, nutrition, and management of liver cancer and varices. The word “improved” cannot replace a specific follow-up testing and safety plan.

Guideline update: ADQI-ICA 2024 recommends against routinely requiring 48 hours of albumin administration before diagnosing HRS-AKI; volume management should be guided by clinical assessment. Debate continues in this area, and scoring is not tied to a single rigid protocol.

## Patient role guidelines

The Patient may describe symptoms, recent medication additions, diet, urine output, and known medical history, but should not volunteer technical diagnoses or terms such as “SBP, SAAG, HRS.” Provide truthful information when asked; do not conceal preset facts to increase difficulty.

## Supervising role conventions

Practice mode may provide graded hints on request about what evidence is still missing; examination mode does not disclose the standard diagnosis or future test results in advance. Feedback is based only on information available at the time.

## Acceptable variation

Antibiotic selection may vary with local resistance patterns, community versus hospital onset, and prior exposure. Albumin dosing strategies and timing of diuretic resumption may vary across regional protocols. Require a rationale and monitoring; do not mark an answer wrong solely because it differs from this reference prescription.

## Medication catalog and actual medication use
